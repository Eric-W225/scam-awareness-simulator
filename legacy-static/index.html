Warning Signs in This Email

    Why Scammers Use Urgency and Fear

    Criminals know that when people feel rushed or afraid, they stop thinking critically. Phrases like "immediate action required" or threats of account suspension are designed to bypass your natural caution. By creating a false deadline, scammers hope you'll click before verifying anything.

    How to Verify Real IRS Communications

    • The IRS does not initiate contact about refunds or account issues by email, text, or social media.
    • Never click links in unsolicited messages. Instead, go directly to irs.gov by typing the address yourself.
    • Check the sender's email address carefully — official IRS emails end in @irs.gov, not look-alike domains.
    • When in doubt, call the IRS using the number listed on irs.gov/contact — not a number from the email.
    • Report suspicious IRS-related emails to phishing@irs.gov (the real reporting address).
